# **Evidence-Based Web Design and Narrative Visualization: Informing the EDAgent Architecture**

## **1\. Executive Synthesis: Separating Authorship, Genericness, and Quality**

The intersection of generative artificial intelligence, web design, and data journalism presents a complex landscape where perceived authorship, structural genericness, and objective user comprehension frequently diverge. An exhaustive analysis of human-computer interaction (HCI) literature, applied user experience (UX) research, and practitioner discourse reveals that the aesthetic markers of AI-generated websites are largely superficial. In contrast, the structural homogenization of the web is a deeper, pre-existing phenomenon accelerated by modern design systems, while actual information comprehension relies on entirely distinct architectural principles.  
Perceived AI authorship is primarily driven by "vibe coding" artifacts—superficial visual cues such as default purple gradients, excessive sparkles, interchangeable pseudo-inspirational copy, and fabricated human personas (e.g., "Sarah Chen" testimonials)1. These markers trigger immediate skepticism, particularly among developer and design communities, who associate them with low-effort production, a lack of architectural rigor, and the absence of quality control2. The phenomenon of design fixation exacerbates this; when generative AI tools are used during the ideation phase, creators unconsciously mimic the AI's suggestions, thereby reducing divergent thinking and propagating identical visual aesthetics across the digital ecosystem4. However, empirical evaluations demonstrate that when these overt stylistic giveaways are removed, users struggle to distinguish AI-generated prototypes from human-made ones, basing their quality judgments instead on pragmatic usability and structural clarity6. The perception of AI authorship is thus a reaction to specific, easily identifiable aesthetic choices rather than an intrinsic detection of machine generation.  
Perceived genericness is a distinct phenomenon rooted in the widespread adoption of utility-first CSS frameworks like Tailwind and component libraries like Bootstrap8. Long before the advent of generative AI, the web underwent significant visual homogenization3. Convolutional neural network analyses of web design history confirm that modern sites converge on highly prototypical layouts, such as the 12-column flex grid, standardized dashboard shells, and uniform card architectures10. While this standardization reduces brand distinctiveness and makes sites feel interchangeable across entirely different product categories, it simultaneously lowers extraneous cognitive load, allowing users to navigate familiar interfaces with greater efficiency11. A generic design is not inherently flawed; it is a manifestation of established HCI conventions that prioritize rapid task execution over aesthetic novelty.  
In the context of data journalism and public-facing analytics, quality and outcomes are measured not by aesthetic uniqueness, but by the reader's ability to accurately comprehend, retain, and calibrate trust in the presented data. First impressions are formed within 50 milliseconds, governed heavily by visual complexity (VC) and prototypicality (PT)13. Sites with low visual complexity and high prototypicality are judged as highly appealing and credible13. Once initial trust is established, comprehension is dictated by narrative structure and uncertainty communication. Author-driven structures, such as the "Martini Glass" model—which begins with a tightly controlled narrative sequence before opening into exploratory interactive tools—significantly outperform unstructured dashboards in transferring complex insights to lay audiences16. Conversely, the presentation of statistical uncertainty requires deliberate friction. Standard static error bars frequently lead to deterministic construal errors, whereas animated Hypothetical Outcome Plots (HOPs) allow untrained observers to intuitively judge distributions and data reliability without relying on advanced statistical literacy19.  
Translating these findings to the EDAgent project—a public road-safety investigation utilizing the Western Pennsylvania Regional Data Center (WPRDC) 2004–2024 cumulative crash extract—requires a definitive pivot from a generic, tool-first application shell to a narrative-first editorial experience21. The current deployment relies heavily on dark-mode dashboard conventions and gated access, which obscures the scientific limitations of the fixed dataset22. By adopting a Martini Glass architecture, EDAgent can guide users through crucial data caveats—such as the 2016 KABCO injury-severity definition changes and the 2024 data coverage gaps—before releasing them into the general dataset analyser or the illustrative scenario calculator21. The design must strictly avoid AI "vibe coding" clichés, utilizing instead a highly prototypical, low-complexity layout that builds public trust while explicitly enforcing the project's scientific boundaries, namely the absolute prohibition of site-level historical screening or real-site predictive benefits21.

The synthesis of these markers indicates that visual aesthetics dictate the immediate perception of authorship and trust, while the underlying architecture dictates actual utility and comprehension. The homogenization of web design driven by CSS utility frameworks like Tailwind creates a baseline of "genericness" that users have come to expect8. When generative AI platforms produce websites, they rely heavily on these frameworks, layering them with hyper-specific stylistic choices (glows, staggered animations) to simulate high-end design1. This combination of underlying structural homogeneity and superficial visual flair is what triggers the perception of an "AI-generated" interface6. Conversely, high-quality narrative websites deliberately break from standard dashboard paradigms to control the flow of information, utilizing techniques like scrollytelling to manage cognitive load and ensure readers process crucial context before interacting with the data24.

### **The Architecture of Comprehension**

The comparative analysis reveals that the most consequential difference between a deliberately designed narrative website and a generic application is the architectural entry point. Generic AI outputs and standard template sites default to a "dashboard" paradigm: a sidebar navigation, a top header, and a grid of equally weighted cards8. This layout assumes the user already possesses the domain knowledge required to formulate a query and interpret the results. In contrast, bespoke data journalism utilizes the "Martini Glass" structure identified by Segel and Heer16. The experience begins with a narrow, author-driven narrative—the stem of the glass—where the designer introduces the primary variables, establishes the baseline, and explains vital caveats. Only after this sequential onboarding does the interface widen into a reader-driven exploratory tool17. For a project like EDAgent, dropping a user directly into a dark-mode dashboard shell generates massive extraneous cognitive load11. The user does not intrinsically know that the 2024 illumination data is missing or that the Pennsylvania injury severity standard changed in 201621. An editorial, scrollytelling entry page resolves this by enforcing the chronological comprehension of data limitations before enabling free exploration.  
Furthermore, the handling of uncertainty sharply divides generic templates from bespoke scientific communication. Data journalists operating under newsroom constraints frequently omit uncertainty entirely, fearing it will increase cognitive load or undermine institutional credibility37. When uncertainty is included in generic templates, it typically manifests as static error bars. However, HCI studies confirm that lay audiences systematically misinterpret static error bars, succumbing to "within-the-bar" bias and deterministic construal errors19. Bespoke narrative interfaces leverage experiential uncertainty. Hypothetical Outcome Plots (HOPs), which animate finite individual draws from a distribution, have been proven to help untrained observers accurately judge trends in ambiguous data without requiring advanced statistical literacy20. While EDAgent's current constraints prohibit real-site predictive models, the Illustrative Scenario Calculator must communicate the hypothetical nature of its Crash Modification Factors (CMF)21. Utilizing HOP-style animations or explicit, manipulable sensitivity ranges prevents the user from interpreting the calculator's arithmetic as a deterministic guarantee of real-world safety improvements.
