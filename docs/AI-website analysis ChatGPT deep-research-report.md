# EDAgent UX Research: AI-Looking Cues, Genericness, Reader Outcomes, and Pre-Build Decisions

## Executive synthesis

The strongest finding is that **“looks AI-generated,” “looks generic,” and “works well” are not the same judgment**. They overlap, but the evidence does not support treating any isolated visual feature—rounded cards, gradients, dark mode, soft shadows, large type, sidebars, or even a familiar SaaS composition—as evidence that AI authored a site. Large-scale research found substantial web-design homogenization well before the present generative-AI era: Goree et al. analyzed more than 200,000 snapshots of more than 10,000 websites from 2003–2019 and found declining layout difference, with shared libraries, standardized color choices, mobile constraints, and changing design practice among plausible contributors. Their interviews also highlight the upside of conventions: familiar patterns can make interfaces easier to use. citeturn792734view0 That directly supports the research brief's requirement to separate perceived authorship from genericness. fileciteturn1file3

The controlled evidence that is closest to the modern “AI interface” question points in the same direction. A 2026 study had 92 computing/engineering students evaluate ten human- and AI-created prototypes without being told authorship. Pragmatic qualities such as clarity and efficiency tended to be positive, while originality and innovation were much weaker; one of the highest pragmatic scores belonged to a human prototype, and some AI prototypes were among the stronger overall designs. The study therefore supports **“conventionality can coexist with usability”**, not “AI interfaces are unusable,” and it cannot establish which visual cues cause people to identify authorship because authorship identification itself was not the measured task. It is also a student sample and prototype study rather than a study of public data-journalism websites. citeturn462978view2turn462978view3

Perceived AI authorship is especially vulnerable to **prior information**. In a 2025 CHI experiment with 911 participants, placing AI-content warning labels on social-media material significantly increased belief that the content was AI-generated; label design also changed trust in the label. The stimuli were social-media/deepfake content rather than websites, so transfer is indirect, but this is strong evidence for a methodological rule: if EDAgent asks participants whether a design “looks AI-made,” it should do so **after** comprehension, task, and quality measures and before revealing any AI provenance. citeturn792734view4 This directly validates the brief's requirement for blind authorship review. fileciteturn1file0

The recurring community notion of an “AI look” is better interpreted as a **compound cue of defaultness or incoherence** than as a forensic signature. Recent Hacker News discussion around a disclosed vibe-coded site included both “this still looks AI-generated” judgments and counterarguments that the issue was simply an inconsistent combination of panels, tabs, and other borrowed patterns; commenters also credited extensive human iteration and taste. citeturn104555view0turn104555view1 Another explicitly AI-generated project, Gen, disclosed that no HTML had been manually authored, yet its system prompt intentionally varied design by page purpose—dense and utilitarian for directories, playful for entertainment, minimal for tools—and at least one commenter preferred its attention value to average human-made sites. That is only anecdotal reception, but it is a useful counterexample to the claim that generative provenance necessarily produces one aesthetic. citeturn104555view2turn104555view3

Conversely, contemporary commercial templates independently reproduce many features now described in public discussion as “AI tells.” A current Framer SaaS template explicitly advertises spacious layouts, modern typography, rounded cards, soft blue accents, a strong hero, feature cards, pricing, FAQ, and final CTA; another uses a dark navy/electric-blue gradient hero, floating dashboard mockups, animated counters, card grids, tabs, FAQ and closing CTA. These are documented templates, not evidence of AI generation. citeturn441998search6turn441998search8 The result is an important counterfactual for EDAgent: **a human-made or template-built site can look “AI-ish,” and an AI-assisted site can be deliberately subject-specific**.

First impressions matter, but their evidentiary meaning is narrower than popular summaries imply. Lindgaard et al.'s 2006 experiments used homepage screenshots and found that participants could form repeatable visual-appeal judgments with extremely short exposures; Study 1 used 22 students viewing 100 pages at 500 ms, Study 2 used 31 students, and Study 3 used 40 participants split between 50 ms and 500 ms. The authors explicitly framed the result as visual appeal, not comprehension, trust, authorship, or long-term task success. citeturn816303view0 Tuch et al. later tested 59 participants on 120 company-homepage stimuli at 50/500/1000 ms and another 82 participants at approximately 17/33/50 ms. Lower visual complexity and higher prototypicality tended to improve beauty ratings, with complexity influencing very early judgments. Again, this was aesthetic response to corporate homepages, not evidence that a conventional civic interface is good or bad. citeturn934370view0turn934370view2

Trust and comprehension have a different evidence base. Fogg et al.'s 2001 study of more than 1,400 participants found perceived credibility associated with factors such as ease of use, expertise, trustworthiness and “real-world feel,” while amateurism reduced credibility; the authors themselves note that credibility is a perceived quality and that their method was a questionnaire drawing on cumulative web experience rather than task observation. citeturn792734view3 For EDAgent, therefore, decorative polish should not be used as a substitute for source visibility, exact scope, usable controls, or methodological specificity.

Communicating caveats does **not** appear to require hiding uncertainty to preserve trust. Van der Bles et al. ran five experiments with a combined 5,780 participants, including a BBC field experiment. Numerical uncertainty made uncertainty more apparent while producing little or no substantive loss of source trust; small negative effects were concentrated more in explicit verbal uncertainty than numerical ranges. The work concerns public facts and estimates rather than crash-record coverage, but it is unusually direct evidence against the fear that honest qualification automatically undermines credibility. citeturn462978view0turn462978view1 For EDAgent this strengthens the case for putting coverage, denominators, coded-era boundaries and missingness close to the claim they qualify rather than burying them in a methods page.

Narrative order also deserves attention independently of visual style. Hullman et al. analyzed 42 professional narrative visualizations and experimentally tested sequencing. One study involved 143 Mechanical Turk participants across 875 trials; another recruited 82 master's students, retaining 73 after a verification screen. Some sequence effects appeared in preference/memory, while hypothesized accuracy differences were not statistically significant in the second experiment. This is evidence that sequencing can matter, but not that a single “scrollytelling” order is universally superior. citeturn816303view2 The Pudding's *Life After Death on Wikipedia* supplies a useful applied example: it defines its adjusted pageview measure before asking readers to interpret it, establishes an ordinary Beyoncé baseline, then introduces the extraordinary Prince spike, expands to a larger set, and only later moves into longer-term patterns. citeturn915140view0 The transferable decision is **baseline → comparison → qualification → broader interpretation**, not “copy The Pudding's visual style.”

For EDAgent, the central release decision in the supplied audit—**make the Allegheny County investigation the public entry point while retaining a task-oriented analyser separately**—is well supported at the information-architecture level. The audit itself distinguishes the three reader goals and proposes public investigation, record, map/evidence, methods, hypothetical scenario, and analyser routes. fileciteturn1file7 fileciteturn1file9 The scientific constraints in the supplied Version 3 plan make that separation especially valuable: its research record is explicitly descriptive and evidence-bounded, keeps real site-level prediction/recommendation out of scope, and treats scenario arithmetic as hypothetical. The most important disagreement with a purely visual diagnosis of the current Overview is that the pictured problem is **not primarily an “AI vibe” problem**. According to the audit's direct inspection of `Screenshot 2026-11-13 164315.png` at 1535×722, the page displays “Reviewed data unavailable,” leaving the shell and blank area to dominate. The same audit says the image alone cannot identify which release read or verifier condition caused the failure. fileciteturn1file5 If reviewed data became available while the shell remained unchanged, the page could become substantially more useful because evidence would exist to read. If the design changed while reviewed data remained unavailable, a better pending-state explanation and methods-oriented entry could improve orientation, but not restore findings. The full public experience therefore requires **both evidence availability and editorial hierarchy**. That is a testable competing-hypothesis model, not a causal diagnosis.

The calculator has a similarly bounded diagnosis. The audit's direct screenshot inspection of `Screenshot 2026-11-13 164337.png` at 1535×862 found a reference selector and long unfilled form with no worked result visible; the untouched state provides no evidence that the calculation itself is unreliable. fileciteturn1file5 The plausible UX hypothesis is that the initial state imposes too much specification before users see what the tool does. The proposed one-click invented example is supported by general form/task principles and by comparison with tools that establish purpose before asking for inputs, but **EDAgent-specific improvement must be tested**, especially because a prominent preset could be misread as an observed county result unless its hypothetical status is unmistakable.

Finally, the analyser should not be forced into the investigation's editorial idiom. Application shells are conventional because persistent navigation, state, datasets, jobs and tools are genuinely useful in task-driven software. Homogenization research explicitly warns that similarity and usability are not opposites. citeturn792734view0 The research therefore supports **differentiation by purpose**: editorial, evidence-first public reading; a small, unmistakably hypothetical calculator; and a conventional, stateful workspace for general data analysis.
